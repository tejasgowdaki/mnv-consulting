What is the Registrar of Companies (ROC)? A Simple Guide

If you run a company or LLP in India, or are planning to start one, you will frequently come across the term ROC — Registrar of Companies. Understanding what the ROC is and what it does is essential for anyone in business in India.

What is the ROC?

The Registrar of Companies (ROC) is a government authority functioning under the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA). It is responsible for administering the Companies Act, 2013 and the Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008 across India.

Every state has its own ROC office that manages companies and LLPs registered within its jurisdiction. For example, companies registered in Karnataka fall under the ROC, Bangalore office.

What Does the ROC Do?

1. Company and LLP Registration

The ROC is the authority that officially registers your company or LLP. Once registered, it issues a Certificate of Incorporation — the document that gives your business a legal identity.

2. Maintaining a Public Register

The ROC maintains a database of all registered companies and LLPs in India. Basic information such as directors, shareholding, registered address, and financial details are publicly accessible on the MCA21 portal ( mca.gov.in ).

3. Receiving and Processing Filings

Every company and LLP must file various documents with the ROC at regular intervals — including annual returns, financial statements, changes in directors or shareholders, and more.

4. Ensuring Compliance

The ROC monitors whether companies are meeting their statutory obligations. If a company fails to file returns or comply with the Companies Act, the ROC can issue notices, impose penalties, and even strike off the company from the register.

5. Striking Off Non-Compliant Companies

If a company has not been carrying on business and has not filed returns for years, the ROC can initiate strike-off proceedings — effectively dissolving the company from the records.

Why is ROC Compliance Important?

  • Legal standing: A company that is struck off loses its legal identity and cannot enter into contracts, open bank accounts, or operate
  • Personal liability: If directors fail to comply, they can face disqualification and personal penalties
  • Creditworthiness: Lenders and investors often check MCA filings before extending credit or making investments
  • Reputation: A clean compliance record on the MCA portal builds trust with stakeholders

Key Filings with the ROC

Form Purpose
Form AOC-4 Filing of financial statements (due 30 days from AGM)
Form MGT-7 Annual return (due 60 days from AGM)
Form DIR-3 KYC KYC of directors
Form ADT-1 Appointment of auditor

The MCA21 Portal

All ROC filings are done digitally through the MCA21 portal ( mca.gov.in ). You can also search for any company's filing history, check director details, and verify a company's status through this portal.

Final Thoughts

The ROC is not just a registration authority — it is an ongoing compliance regulator throughout the life of your company. Staying on top of your ROC filings ensures your company remains in good standing and avoids costly penalties.
